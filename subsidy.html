PM Surya Ghar + Odisha OASBY

In Odisha you can get up to ₹1,38,000 towards your solar system

₹78,000 from the central PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, plus up to ₹60,000 more from the Odisha state scheme on top. Both are paid after your system is commissioned. Here is exactly how much you get, whether you qualify, and what the process actually looks like.

Jump to the Odisha rules
Step 1

How much will you get?

The subsidy is calculated per kilowatt, and it caps out at 3 kW. A larger system is still worth building — you just don't get extra central subsidy above ₹78,000.

1 kW₹30,000₹900 – ₹1,200120 units / month
2 kW₹60,000₹1,800 – ₹2,500240 units / month

4 kW₹78,000 (capped)₹3,800 – ₹4,800480 units / month

The exact formula: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW, then ₹18,000 for the third kW. So 1 kW = ₹30,000 · 2 kW = ₹60,000 · 3 kW = ₹60,000 + ₹18,000 = ₹78,000. Nothing further is paid beyond 3 kW.
Group housing societies and RWAs follow a different rate: ₹18,000 per kW for solar on common facilities — lifts, water pumps, corridor and compound lighting — up to a limit of 500 kW, calculated at 3 kW per house.
Good news if you're in Odisha: the state pays an additional subsidy on top of everything above, which can take your total support to around ₹1,38,000 for a 3 kW system. See the Odisha rules below →

Step 2

Do you qualify?

Most Indian households do. All five of these need to be true:

  • You are an Indian citizen with a residential electricity connection
  • The electricity connection is in the applicant's name, and the bank account for the subsidy is in the same name
  • You own the roof, or have written permission from the owner to install on it
  • Your sanctioned load is 10 kW or below (higher loads need a load revision first)
  • There is no existing rooftop solar that has already claimed a central subsidy at this connection

Who does not get this subsidy

  • Commercial and industrial connections. Businesses instead get 40% accelerated depreciation in year one, which is often worth more. Ask us to model it.
  • Systems using non-DCR panels. Both cells and module must be made in India. This is why the DCR tag on our catalogue matters.
  • Systems installed before applying. You must have discom feasibility approval before installation. Installing first and applying later disqualifies you.
  • Off-grid systems with no net meter. The subsidy requires a grid-connected, net-metered installation.

Low-interest loan option

Collateral-free loans are available through participating banks for residential systems up to 3 kW, at concessional interest rates. For many households the monthly loan instalment is smaller than the electricity bill it replaces. We can point you to the lenders active in your area.

The criteria for availing a loan are as per bank norms. Eligibility, sanction and terms are decided by the bank, not by us.

Step 4

The application, start to finish

This is the full official process. We do steps 2 through 8 for you — you handle the registration and the final bank details, because those must be in your own name.

Register on the National Portal

Go to pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Select your state and discom, enter your consumer number, mobile number and email. Verify with the OTP.

Submit the rooftop application

Log in with your consumer number, fill in the proposed system size and upload your electricity bill. We prepare this with you so the sizing matches your sanctioned load.

Wait for discom feasibility approval

Your discom checks that the local transformer and your connection can take the system. Typically a few days to a couple of weeks. Do not install before this arrives.

Choose a registered vendor

The installation must be done by a vendor registered with your discom on the portal, using ALMM-listed DCR modules. Check our registration before you sign anything — with us or anyone else.

Installation

Mounting structure, panels, inverter, AC/DC cabling, earthing and lightning arrestor. Usually two to four days for a residential system.

Apply for the net meter

Submit the installation details on the portal and apply for net metering. The discom inspects the installation and installs a bi-directional meter.

Commissioning certificate

After a successful inspection the discom issues a commissioning certificate, which is generated on the portal. This is the document that triggers your subsidy.

Submit bank details — subsidy arrives

Upload your cancelled cheque and bank details on the portal. The subsidy is credited to your account by direct benefit transfer, typically within 30 to 45 days of commissioning.

🌴 Odisha

Odisha pays a second subsidy on top of the central one

Most states leave you with just the central ₹78,000. Odisha does not. Under the Odisha Akshaya Shakti Bikash Yojana (OASBY), approved by the state cabinet in January 2025, the state adds its own assistance on top — and it changes the economics of a home system substantially.

Please read this before you count on the figure. The Odisha government has publicly committed to additional state assistance of up to ₹60,000 for residential systems up to 3 kW, and has budgeted for it — ₹890 crore in 2025–26 and a further ₹495 crore in the 2026–27 state budget, targeting around three lakh households. The exact per-slab split has been reported slightly differently in different places, and state schemes are revised from time to time. We confirm the precise amount that applies to your application with your discom before it goes into your quotation. You can also verify directly with OREDA on 0674-2588260.

How the two subsidies work together

They are separate payments from separate budgets, and both arrive after your system is commissioned:

  • You apply once, through the national portal — there is no separate OASBY application form to chase
  • The central subsidy is paid by the Government of India by direct benefit transfer
  • The state assistance is released through the state's own channel against the same commissioned installation
  • Both require the same things: a DCR module, a registered vendor, discom approval and a net meter

The two payments do not always land on the same day. The central amount usually arrives first.

Why this matters more than people realise

On a 3 kW system costing around ₹1,74,000, the combined support of roughly ₹1,38,000 brings your out-of-pocket cost down to somewhere near ₹36,000.

Against a ₹3,000 monthly bill, that pays back in roughly a year — and then generates free electricity for another twenty-four.

There are very few investments available to an Odia household with that return profile.

Which distribution company handles your application?

Odisha has four discoms, all joint ventures between Tata Power (51%) and the Government of Odisha (49%). Your application, feasibility approval and net meter all go through whichever one serves your area — and the name is printed at the top of your electricity bill, so you never have to guess.

Central Odisha, across nine districts — including Khordha (Bhubaneswar), Cuttack and Puri.

TPNODL

Balasore, Mayurbhanj, Bhadrak, Jajpur and Keonjhar.

TPWODL

TP Western Odisha Distribution Ltd

TPSODL

TP Southern Odisha Distribution Ltd

Southern Odisha, including the Berhampur and Ganjam belt and the Koraput–Rayagada region.

OERC rules

Net metering in Odisha, specifically

Net metering is regulated by the Odisha Electricity Regulatory Commission (OERC), not by the central government. These are the rules that actually apply to your roof.

System size limit

Net metering is available from 1 kWp up to 500 kWp, or up to your sanctioned load — whichever is lower. OERC removed the older 1 MW upper cap. For a home this almost never binds; your sanctioned load is the real constraint.

The transformer rule

Total solar connected to your local distribution transformer cannot exceed 75% of that transformer's capacity. This is the usual reason a feasibility application gets held up — if many neighbours have already gone solar, the local transformer may be full.

No feasibility study up to 10 kW

For systems of 10 kW and below, no separate technical feasibility study is required. This covers virtually every home installation and is why residential approvals in Odisha move relatively quickly.

How surplus units are settled

Excess generation is carried forward from one billing cycle to the next, through to the end of the financial year. Your bill each month is raised on the net import after adjusting the carried-forward credit.

The 90% consumption cap

Over the settlement period, energy credited from your solar system is capped at 90% of your own consumption. In plain terms: net metering in Odisha is designed to wipe out your bill, not to turn your roof into a business. This is why honest sizing matters — an oversized system gives away its surplus.

Hybrid inverters now allowed

A November 2025 amendment to the OERC net metering order permits hybrid inverters and machine-to-machine (M2M) data loggers on net-metered systems. Practically, this means you can now add battery backup without losing net metering — which was not previously possible.

The one that catches people out: the 90% consumption cap combined with the sanctioned-load limit means the biggest system your roof can hold is often not the system you should buy. We size against your actual annual consumption, not your available roof area — and in Odisha that usually saves customers money rather than costing them.

The other half of the saving

Net metering: how surplus power becomes money

A subsidy reduces what you pay upfront. Net metering is what makes the system keep paying you for twenty-five years — and it is the part most people misunderstand.

Your solar panels generate most in the middle of the day, when a typical home is using the least. Without net metering, all that surplus is simply wasted. With a net meter — a bi-directional meter your discom installs — the surplus flows back into the grid and is credited to your account in units.

At the end of the billing cycle, you are billed only on the net: units drawn from the grid minus units exported to it. Many households end up paying nothing more than the fixed connection charge.

Worth knowing: Net metering rules — how long credits carry forward, what happens to unused credits at year end, and the maximum system size allowed — are set by each state electricity regulator, not by the central government. In Odisha that is the OERC, and we've set out its specific rules in the Odisha section above.

Net metering vs gross metering vs net billing

  • Net metering — you are billed on the difference between import and export, unit for unit. Best for homes, and the standard for residential rooftop under this scheme.
  • Gross metering — everything you generate is sold to the discom at a fixed rate, and you buy everything you consume at the retail tariff. Usually worse for households.
  • Net billing — export is credited in rupees at a rate lower than the retail tariff. Some states apply this above certain system sizes.
Avoid these

Why subsidy applications get stuck

Almost every delayed application we have seen traces back to one of these.

✕ Mistake

Installing the system before discom feasibility approval comes through.

✓ Do this

Wait for written feasibility approval on the portal. It usually takes days, not months, and skipping it voids the claim entirely.

✕ Mistake

Electricity connection in a parent's or late relative's name, bank account in yours.

✓ Do this

Get the names matched first — either transfer the connection or use a bank account in the connection holder's name. Fixing this after commissioning is slow.

✕ Mistake

Using cheaper non-DCR imported panels because a vendor said the subsidy still applies.

✓ Do this

Insist on ALMM-listed DCR modules and keep the serial numbers. Ask to see the model on the ALMM list before installation begins.

✕ Mistake

Sizing the system above the sanctioned load without revising the load first.

✓ Do this

Check your sanctioned load on your bill. If your desired system exceeds it, apply for a load enhancement with the discom before the solar application.

✕ Mistake

Paying a vendor who promises to "adjust the subsidy in the price" on a residential system.

✓ Do this

Under this scheme the subsidy is paid to you by direct benefit transfer, after commissioning. Anyone claiming otherwise is either confused or not being straight with you.
