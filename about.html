Our story

Why we started

Rooftop solar is one of the few things an Indian household can buy that pays for itself and then keeps paying. That is precisely why the market around it filled up so quickly with people selling it badly — inflated quotations, B-grade modules passed off as new, subsidy figures quoted as though they were guaranteed, and installers who stop answering the phone the week after commissioning.

Bright Solar Engineering was set up to do the unglamorous version of this business properly. We are an authorised dealer for Waaree and V-Guard, and we install what we sell. We carry two brands instead of ten deliberately: a dealer who stocks whatever is cheapest this month cannot tell you how a module actually ages in coastal heat and salt air, and cannot get a warranty claim moving when one fails.

That view comes from a long time in the trade. Both partners came to solar after full careers elsewhere — one after thirty-five years in electricals and sales, most of it with Havells; the other after forty-five years in automobile sales, retiring as a General Manager with Mahindra. Eighty years between them of selling equipment, standing behind it, and running the people who service it. It teaches you which corners get cut, which specifications quietly matter, and what a customer discovers two years after the installer has gone.

Most of the difference between a system that still makes its numbers in year twenty and one that quietly underperforms from year two is decided before anything is bolted down — in the shadow study, the string layout, the structure specification and the earthing. None of it is visible from the road, and none of it appears as a line you can compare between two quotations. That is the part we take seriously.

We supply across the state from our showroom in Pipili, and our own installation and service teams treat the surrounding districts as their daily ground. For a site further out we will tell you plainly, before you commit, how long a service call will take to reach you. A warranty is only ever as good as the distance between you and the person honouring it, and we would rather you knew that number in advance than discovered it in year four.

The parts other installers skip

Most of what separates a good solar installation from a bad one is invisible from the ground. These are the things we do not cut.

A real shadow analysis

We check where shadows fall across the whole day and across seasons — water tanks, parapets, neighbouring buildings, trees that will grow. Then we lay out the strings around them.

Structure rated for your wind zone

Hot-dip galvanised or anodised aluminium, engineered for local wind loading, with correct spacing for airflow beneath the panels. Not the lightest section that will pass a glance.

Proper earthing and surge protection

Separate earth pits for array, inverter and lightning arrestor, with surge protection on both DC and AC sides. This is the single most commonly omitted item in cheap quotations.

Handover you can actually use

A file with your single-line diagram, serial numbers, warranty documents, commissioning certificate, net metering agreement and a walkthrough of the monitoring app.

The technical bit

What a compliant system actually has to meet

Rooftop solar in India is a regulated product, not just hardware on a roof. These are the rules your installation has to satisfy — worth knowing, because not one of them is visible in a price.

Modules must be on ALMM List-I

MNRE's Approved List of Models and Manufacturers is the gate for anything government-assisted. If your module's exact model is not on List-I, your subsidy is refused — the panel may generate perfectly well, but the money will not arrive. We put the model number on the quotation so you can check it against the published list yourself.

Since June 2026, the cells must be Indian too

A List-I module now has to be built from cells on ALMM List-II — cells actually manufactured in India, not imported and laminated here. That took effect on 1 June 2026 for new subsidised residential systems, with no exemption. It is why some panels quietly disappeared from dealer price lists this year.

DCR is not optional for subsidy

Domestic Content Requirement means the cells and the module are made in India. Non-DCR panels are usually cheaper and entirely legal — they simply disqualify you from PM Surya Ghar altogether. If a quotation looks unusually cheap, this is the first thing to check, not the last.

The inverter has to be BIS certified

Grid-tied inverters are certified to IS 16221, which follows the international IEC 62109 safety standard. The two requirements that matter to you: anti-islanding that disconnects within two seconds, and total harmonic distortion held under 5%. Anti-islanding is a safety interlock — it stops your roof energising a line a lineman believes is dead.

CEA standards, and your discom's approval

Grid connection is governed by CEA technical standards for voltage, frequency and harmonics. In practice that means technical feasibility approval from your discom before installation, and its inspection before the net meter goes on. Nobody can shortcut this, and anyone offering to is selling you a problem for later.

Structures designed to the coastal wind zone

IS 875 (Part 3) sets the wind loading a structure must survive, and coastal Odisha sits in one of the highest bands in the country — with a further importance factor applied in cyclone-prone coastal districts. A structure specified for an inland site is lighter, cheaper and wrong here. Fani and Amphan settled that argument locally.

How to check any solar dealer — including us

You should not have to take our word for any of the above. Four questions separate a serious installer from a confident salesman, and you can ask them of us on the phone today.

  • "What is the exact module model number?" Then look it up on the ALMM List-I yourself. A dealer who will not give you the model number before you pay has told you something.
  • "Are you empanelled with my discom, or only registered nationally?" Vendor registration is two layers — the National Portal first, then each discom separately. A vendor registered nationally but not empanelled with yours cannot complete your subsidy file. Odisha has four distribution companies — TPCODL, TPNODL, TPWODL and TPSODL — and which one is yours depends on where in the state you live.
  • "Can I see the itemised bill of materials?" Structure, DC and AC cabling, earthing pits, surge protection and the meter box are where a cheap quotation hides its savings. A single lump-sum figure is not a quotation.
  • "What happens in year four?" Who comes out, how quickly, and at whose cost. Ask for the answer in a sentence with a number in it. Ours depends on how far you are from our showroom, and we would rather quote you an honest two days than a cheerful "same day" we cannot keep.
